Try to tickle yourself. Nothing, right? 🪶

Your brain predicts the touch before it happens. And what's predicted feels weaker.

Researchers built a robot that stroked people's palm, controlled by their own other hand. When the robot added a tiny delay, just a fraction of a second, it started to tickle.

Your brain cancels out its own surprises. That's why you can't tickle yourself. And why someone else's touch always feels stronger.

Not just you. 🧡

Source: Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert (1999), Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience
