Monday, 1900. Anna gets up before dawn. Today is wash day, and it will take her the whole day. 🧺

One load of laundry needs about 50 gallons of water. Every drop, carried in by hand.
She hauls it from the pump, heats it on the stove, scrubs every shirt on a washboard, boils it, wrings it, hangs it out.
In one year she walks about 150 miles just carrying water. That's 36 tons.

And the work isn't over: Tuesday is ironing day, with heavy irons heated on that same stove.
Then came running water, electricity and the washing machine.

Today you press one button, and you don't even have to watch.

What would you do with your Mondays back? 👇

Not just you (Anna is illustrative, the numbers are for an American household around 1900. The washerwomen film is from France, 1897.)
