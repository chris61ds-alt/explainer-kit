Paris, 1838. A busy boulevard: carriages, horses, people everywhere. But in the very first photo of it, the street is empty. 📷

The picture needed minutes of light. Everything that moved simply vanished. Except one man, standing still to get his boots shined.

That's how you got into a photo: you froze. Studios even put iron stands behind your head. Blink, and your face became a blur.
By the 1850s it took just a few seconds. People still didn't smile: a photo was rare, expensive and forever. Some were even told to say "prunes" to keep the mouth small.

Then, in 1900, Kodak's Brownie camera cost one dollar. Suddenly everyone took snapshots, and the smile moved in.
Today, your phone takes a photo in less than a hundredth of a second.

Would you have smiled for a photo that lasts forever? 👇

Not just you (The street film is from 1897 and only illustrates a busy Paris street. The exposure time of Daguerre's 1838 photo is estimated at several minutes.)
