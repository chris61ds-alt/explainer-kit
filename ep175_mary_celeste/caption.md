December 4, 1872. A ship drifts across the Atlantic, sails up, and nobody is on board. 🚢

Ten people gone: Captain Benjamin Briggs, his wife Sarah, their 2-year-old daughter Sophia and seven crew.
The ship could still sail. Six months of food. Their things still there.
But the lifeboat was gone, one pump was taken apart, and there was water in the hold.

The newspapers wrote "pirates". Others blamed mutiny, a sea monster, even something from the sky. A young Arthur Conan Doyle wrote a made-up story about it, and many readers believed it.
A court in Gibraltar found no sign of a crime.

The most likely idea: Briggs feared the ship was sinking or the alcohol might explode, and took everyone into the lifeboat. It was never found.
