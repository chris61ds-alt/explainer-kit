You remember their face. Their job. Their dog. But not their name. 🙃

In one experiment, people saw a face and were told: "this man is a baker." Others were told: "his name is Baker."

Same word. But the job was remembered far better than the name.

It's called the Baker/baker paradox. A baker comes with bread, flour and ovens. A name like Baker comes with nothing to hold on to.

So give names a hook. Picture Mr. Baker holding a loaf of bread. 🍞

Not just you.

Sources: McWeeny, Young, Hay & Ellis (1987), British Journal of Psychology · Cohen (1990), British Journal of Psychology
