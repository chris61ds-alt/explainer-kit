Your friend's success stings. That doesn't make you a bad friend. 🫣

In a classic experiment, men brought a friend to a word-guessing game. They could give easy clues or hard ones.

Then the researchers said the game measured intelligence. And they gave their friend harder clues than a stranger. In 10 of 13 sessions.

When it's not your thing, you cheer for your friend. When it is your thing, their win can feel like your loss.

What helps: celebrate anyway. In studies on good news, an excited, curious reply went with closer relationships.

So feel the sting. Then say: tell me everything.

Tag a friend you're proud of. 👇

Not just you (The 1980 experiment was small and only included men. When the game was described as unimportant, friends got the easier clues. The good-news studies show a link, not proof.)

Sources: Tesser & Smith (1980), "Some Effects of Task Relevance and Friendship on Helping", Journal of Experimental Social Psychology · Gable, Reis, Impett & Asher (2004), "What Do You Do When Things Go Right?", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
