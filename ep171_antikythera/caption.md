1900. Sponge divers shelter from a storm off a tiny Greek island. One diver comes back up terrified: he saw bodies on the seabed. 🌊

They were bronze statues, from a shipwreck 2,000 years old. And among them lay a lump of corroded bronze.

Two years later, someone spotted a tiny, hand-cut gear inside it. Nothing that complex would be built for another thousand years. Some said: aliens.
For a century, nobody could see what was inside. Then, in 2005, X-ray scans looked through the bronze.
What they found was no alien tech: more than 30 bronze gears, and tiny Greek writing, like a user manual.

The lump was a machine. Turn a handle, and it showed the sun and the moon and predicted eclipses. It was built by Greeks, around 150–100 BC.

Today, scientists call it the oldest known analog computer.

No aliens. Just a lump of corroded bronze, and Greek genius. ⚙️
