The year is 1800. Night falls, and Mary wants her room as bright as yours, for just one hour. 🕯️

That one hour of light will cost her almost six hours of work.
Her light comes from tallow candles, made of animal fat. To make a room as bright as yours, she would need about 80 of them, burning all at once.

For five hundred years, the price of light barely moved. So for most people, the day simply ended at sunset.
Then, in less than a century: gas lamps, kerosene and the light bulb.

Today, the same hour of light costs you less than half a second of work.

What would you do with six extra hours of evening? 👇

Not just you (Mary is illustrative. "As bright as yours" = 1,000 lumen-hours, about one modern bulb for an hour. Labour times are economist William Nordhaus' estimates.)
